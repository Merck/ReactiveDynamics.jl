The problem

Decisions about how an operation behaves need a model, and a model needs a modeler

  • A launch reservation, a bid under a staffing ceiling, a pipeline whose funding follows its results, a backlog that feeds back into demand: the answer depends on how the operation behaves over time.
  • A workbook can hold a time series and even a Monte Carlo draw. It cannot easily hold one shared line allocated month by month between products with random launch dates, plus a rule that changes the plan when a readout lands. That takes a simulator nobody can check.
  • So these questions go to a specialist, take weeks, and come back as a workbook the decision-maker has to take on faith.
  • Most never get a model at all.

The value

More decisions get a real model, the owner stays in the loop, the answer travels

  • A model for the cost of a conversation. The threshold for a real model drops from a specialist's weeks to a guided session with the decision owner.
  • The owner stays in the loop. The interview runs in business language with the person who owns the decision. Every assumption lands in a brief they signed, including which inputs were their own estimates.
  • The answer travels. A study ends in a generated report the modeler can forward: the answer, the model, the evidence and the provenance, laid out to one template.
  • It compounds. The next question about the same operation starts from the same model.

The unique value

The model is data, not code, so it can be checked before it runs

Static checks. A pure validator reads the model document and returns every problem with its exact location. A malformed model produces diagnostics, not a plausible wrong number. The language model proposes, a deterministic checker disposes, and the loop repeats until the checker is silent.

The model is never code. It is a closed vocabulary the engine compiles; nothing in a document can name a program.

Where it stops today. In the case, the odds-weighting behind the headline ran in session-written scripts: published, re-run independently, but reviewed by reading. Moving it into rdx effect is W27.

The model document. Places that hold resources, transitions that hold them over time and sometimes fail, rates that read the live state, rules that decide mid-course, costs, what each option changes. Diffable, and replayable from the model and a seed.

Checked again, for meaning. Plausibility checks ask whether the model does what the business does: a bottleneck that never binds, a step that never fires, a horizon that cuts off the payoff.

Guarded in CI. The schema reference is generated from the engine, and a known-answer test re-derives a published result on every change.

The case · Author + Diagnose

One model, a version per option, checked against the operator's own figures

  • A generator script writes one network; the options differ only in parameters.
  • rdx validate: 0 errors on every document.
  • Consistency check: Kestrel first-year revenue $250.0M against the operator's ~$250M, and 54 batches a year at peak. Both follow from the inputs by construction; they show the inputs went in right.
  • rdx diagnose: the own line binds. Every option failed one check (steps that never fire on a single run); DOCTOR.md explains each before any number is reported.
  • Options compared on paired paths: the same seed gives every option the same readout dates and lead times.
  • Readout outcomes are forced per path and weighted by the stated odds (0.27 / 0.33 / 0.18 / 0.22).
  • No option-level ensembles were run: a 96-seed ensemble took 53 minutes (W24). One wait ensemble served as a cross-check, and replays of three of its members matched exactly.

The case · What gets shared

The study ships as a generated report, not a folder

Six parts, one template, every study:

  1. The answer and the decision rule
  2. The evidence, sensitivities and caveats
  3. Why a spreadsheet cannot produce it
  4. The model: elements in the operator's words, and the diff that is the option
  5. The execution map
  6. How we got it: commands, commit, the seeds

Generated, never edited. render_report.jl builds it from the study's own files, so it cannot drift from them. Its prose is the study's own FINDINGS, written by the model; a critic that audits prose against numbers is W9.

Self-contained. One HTML file with a PDF beside it; every figure embedded, nothing to open in the repository.

Checkable. It names the commit, hashes both model documents, and lists all 208 stored runs by document and seed: any number can be replayed.

Honest about it

What we are not claiming

  • The case is simulated, AI on both sides. The operator was cooperative. It demonstrates the workflow, not anyone's supply plan.
  • Paired paths, not ensembles. A 96-seed ensemble took 53 minutes (W24); every figure says what it rests on.
  • The interview left soft inputs untested: demand ±30%, marketed growth and readout correlation. The published audit leads with them.
  • The prose is not yet checked. The assistant retracted two of its own claims out loud; a critic that does this systematically is W9.
  • Contention is checked end to end; feedback and coupling are not yet (W25).
  • The outlined pieces are the direction, not shipped.
