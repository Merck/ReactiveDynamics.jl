# `agent/sessions/` — captured rd-study sessions

A study's four artifacts (`BRIEF.md`, `model.rdj.json`, `DOCTOR.md`, `FINDINGS.md`) show you what the workflow *produced*. They do not show you what it is actually like to use, which is the thing a prospective user needs to see: which questions get asked, which defaults get proposed, what happens when the user's numbers contradict the assistant's arithmetic, and how a business question turns into a document with a validator behind it. This directory holds transcripts of whole sessions, rendered as single-file HTML so they can be mailed, committed, or opened in five years without a build step.

```bash
julia --project=. agent/scripts/render_session.jl agent/sessions/<name>.json
# → agent/sessions/<name>.html — self-contained: no external CSS, fonts, or JS; SVGs inlined
```

The transcript JSON is the source of truth and is meant to be read as diffable text; the HTML is generated and safe to regenerate. The schema is documented in the header of `agent/scripts/render_session.jl`: a `title`/`subtitle`/`headline` block, a `meta` map rendered as the provenance strip, a `hero` skim card of `stats`/`text`/`figures`, `turns[]` — each with a `role` (`user` / `assistant` / `note`), the turn text, an optional `phase` label that starts a new band, an optional `tools[]` of `{cmd, out}` pairs rendered as a collapsed evidence block, and `figures[]` that get inlined — and `appendix[]` for the deliverables, where an entry takes `"file"` in place of `"text"` to import a study's Markdown report at render time.

A transcript is the spine of a page, not necessarily the whole of one. `sections[]` puts authored sheets *before* the dialogue with the same block grammar as the appendix, and `turns` may be `{"file": "<another page>.json"}` instead of a list — which imports another document's transcript rather than copying it. [`agent/overview.html`](../overview.html) is that shape: a capability overview whose demonstration is the `portfolio_bid` session below, so the session has exactly one representation under version control and still stands alone as its own page.

## Two provenances, and what each one is worth

A transcript is only worth showing if it was not written to flatter the thing it demonstrates. Two kinds live in this directory, they are worth different things, and the provenance strip at the top of each rendered page says which one you are reading.

A **scripted walkthrough** starts from a finished study: the model is authored, the batteries are run, the figures are built, and only then is the dialogue composed around those artifacts. Its discipline is documentary — the study's provenance file (`README.md` in the study directory) records every command and every number, the transcript may quote nothing absent from it, and the appendix *imports* the delivered `FINDINGS.md` at render time instead of restating it, so the page cannot drift from the report. What a walkthrough shows is the shape of the work and the substance of the result, at the scale a real engagement runs at. What it cannot show is that the questions would arrive in that order, or that an interview would recover the facts.

A **blind capture** is the control for exactly that. It is **role-played by two separate agents that cannot see each other's reasoning**, under a protocol designed so that the interesting behaviour cannot be faked:

1. The **operator** agent is given a business situation and, *before speaking a single word*, writes its facts to a file — call volumes, durations, resource counts, contract terms, penalty figures, today's observed backlog. That file is a pre-commitment: the operator must answer from it, and cannot invent a convenient number later to rescue a conclusion.
2. The **assistant** agent starts from an empty context, is pointed at `agent/skills/rd-study/SKILL.md`, and is forbidden from reading the directory the fact sheet lives in. Everything it knows about the business, it elicited.
3. A relay passes messages verbatim in both directions. Neither agent sees the other's tool calls, scratch work, or instructions.

The consequence is the property that makes the transcript evidence rather than marketing: **every number in the model was extracted by asking**, and every number in the result was produced by running the engine. Where the assistant proposed a placeholder and the operator corrected it, you can watch the correction land.

The blind protocol's own fact sheet is committed next to the transcript, with a line-by-line audit of what the interview recovered and what it missed: [`field_service_facts.md`](field_service_facts.md). **Read the audit before the transcript if you are evaluating rather than browsing** — it is where the workflow's weaknesses are, and it names three: the interview took a *range* for a quantity the operator had a point estimate for (which is how the tick-grid defect survived to phase 4), it never modelled the value of a completed repair, and it carried none of the three quantities the operator had explicitly flagged as guesses into the study as sensitivities. The pattern is that the interview was strong on contractual facts and weak on exactly the numbers its informant had labelled soft.

What this does *not* prove: that a real human would answer as fluently as a role-played one, or that a real operation matches the shape the persona described. A captured session demonstrates the workflow, not the state of anyone's field-service department. The header of each rendered page says so. In particular this persona was *cooperative* — it answered in good faith and volunteered its own uncertainty markers — so the capture says little about the harder case of an informant who misremembers or contradicts themselves between sessions.

## Sessions

| Session | Kind | Question | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`portfolio_bid`](portfolio_bid.json) | scripted walkthrough | A VP of corporate development has a term sheet for three clinical-stage assets, a banker's risk-adjusted NPV of about \$740M, and a 40-FTE development group that the company's own pipeline already fills. What is the package worth *to us*, and does the bid have to be paired with integration capacity? | The seller's valuation reproduces inside the model the moment the FTE ceiling is lifted and nothing else changes: **+744 ± 144** \$M of eNPV. The same three assets against the real 40-FTE group deliver **+241 ± 90** — **\$503M ± 169 of the standalone number is stranded in a queue**, and the third asset sits on the shelf 31 months. The second cheque is the one that pays: twelve more FTE are worth **+370 ± 106 given the deal** and **−8 ± 91 without it**. That asymmetry is the finding, and it is not in the assets — it is in the contention, which is why it cannot come out of a spreadsheet. Twenty-four FTE cannot be told apart from twelve, so the cheaper dose wins. The transcript also carries a retraction, and it is not about the model: a summary row had been typed by hand with a value from one contrast and a standard error from another, publishing a clear result as a null. Re-deriving every row from the envelopes caught it before publication — the interaction is **+378 ± 140, 2.71 se**, so the two levers are complements — and the script that does the re-deriving is committed next to the study. |
| [`field_service`](field_service.json) | blind capture | A lab-equipment vendor's field-service manager has a budget meeting in six weeks and three candidate levers: hire 2 more technicians, buy more diagnostic equipment, or change the dispatch priority rule. | The lever the manager arrived asking about ranks worst of the three: 2 technicians avoid \$57,504 ± 21,741 of annual penalties for \$190k/yr, while 2 diagnostic laptops avoid \$228,050 ± 18,712 for \$38k once. The free policy change cannot be separated from zero on any of five metrics at 48 seeds. **Then the operator pushed back** — their own 4.3 h rule of thumb turned out to be unrepresentable on the model's 1-hour tick grid — and the assistant had to find, report and repair a defect in its own model on a finer grid, which moved the laptop estimate *up* to \$288,644 ± 31,502 and retired three published figures. The repair is the most useful part of the transcript. |
